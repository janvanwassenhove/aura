Does the unified console actually work?

An honest pass over D — Unified: what earns its place, what is decoration, and where it quietly reintroduces the problems the original review was written to solve.

Verdict

The boundary system is now genuinely good — modes are visible, legible and consequential, identity is handled honestly, and Stop never leaves the screen. That part I would ship.

The surface switch is the problem. Two complete UIs over one backend means ~60% of the product is built twice, the user carries a mental model they didn't ask for, and the header now offers two identical-looking pill switches where one changes what a robot may do in your house and the other only changes how dense the pixels are. D solves the IA problem inside each surface and creates a new one between them.

What I'd do next: D2, one surface with a density dial

Keep every screen D built, drop the idea that they are two apps.

  • One layout, three densities — Calm / Standard / Full, on a single control that changes how much of the same screen is exposed: Calm hides the log, inspector and mono detail; Full shows everything Workbench does. Progressive disclosure, not a second app.
  • Modes stay exactly as they are — the header switcher, the capability chips, the boundary line. It is the one thing D got unambiguously right.
  • Density follows the person, not a toggle — Noor and Sam get Calm by default, the owner gets Full. Face recognition already knows who is standing there; use it.
  • Every feature has exactly one home — camera, persona, approvals, people each live in one place that grows detail with density, instead of existing twice in two idioms.
  • Visual differentiation by mode, not by surface — Present already recolours the chrome. Let modes own the visual shift; density only changes information volume.

Cost of not doing this: every feature from here on needs two designs, two builds, and a decision about which surface it belongs to — which is exactly the ambiguity finding 02 of the original review was about.
